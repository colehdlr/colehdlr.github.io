I found out about the [Amateur Entomologists' Society's annual convention](https://www.amentsoc.org/events/exhibitions.html) from an employee I'd spoken to the week prior while accompanying my partner to look into buying vampire crabs at a reptile store. 
After doing some research online during the week, the following Saturday morning we took a train to Kempton Park Racecourse, Surrey, and purchased two adult convention tickets.

Entering the convention's main hall, we saw entomologists wandering around with their wooden boxes, and immediately smelt the musty scent of the pinned and live invertebrates from the trading stands.

> Wooden entomology boxes are used to store pinned invertebrates for both transport and long-term storage as part of a larger collection. They're designed to protect against dust, moisture and pests.

For the first hour, we explored the convention, impressed by the variety of beetles, moths, butterflies and tarantulas pinned and organised for their selection by hobbyists. 
Further in, we found live millipedes, centipedes and the prior mentioned invertebrates also for sale as live pets.

![Image](/images/millipedes.png){: width="300" }
*Box of millipedes, including one that I held*

Near the back of the convention we spent some time with [Isolife's stand](https://isolife.co.uk/); as my partner was looking for isopods for her vampire crab paludarium.
The employees were lovely and directed us to their starter kit and a small colony of *Cubaris m. 'Mandarin'* which we bought planning to breed in preparation for supplying the future vampire crabs with a cleaning crew.

![Image](/images/isopod.png){: width="120"}
*One of our new Cubaris m. 'Mandarin'*

I also considered buying one of their millipedes and went to [Venomous Visions' stand](https://www.venomousvisions.co.uk/) to practice handling one to see if I liked it.
Although tempted, I decided it would be best to wait and get a feel for the isopods before committing to a life of invertebrate ownership.

Next on the agenda was finding a pinned Atlas moth as a gift for a birthday party the next day.
These are one of the largest insects on Earth, with a maximum wingspan of 30cm, found in South and Southeast Asia.

Whilst looking for one, we spoke to an entomologist at smaller stand, Andrew Spicer, who told us the story of his discovery in Cameroon a few years back.
It had been previously understood that _Eudaemonia_ (a genus of giant silk moth) did not have mouthparts (proboscis).
However, on a entomology trip with his son, Andrew noticed a _Eudaemonia argiphontes_ drinking water from his hand, surprising him and proving this theory incorrect. 
Later in the year, he also ["observed the _Eudaemonia argiphontes_ would die overnight in captivity unless they were housed in a cage with available surface water"](https://www.cameroonark.org/tales-of-tongues). 
It was awesome to hear about this first-hand, and made me realise that a lot of the people we'd been chatting to that day may also have been researchers or else more involved than we'd have thought.

Moving on, we were able to find a few sellers with Atlas moth's and opted for the cheapest from the smaller, but very friendly, stand run by [Bob Dollery](https://www.amentsoc.org/about/news/0257/) upstairs.

![Image](/images/atlasMoth.png){: width="260"}
*Behold, the Atlas moth*

After being in the convention for over 3 hours, we grabbed a burger and chips from the stand outside and headed back. 

All in all, both the attendees and the stand owners were very chatty and enthusiastic, and everyone we spoke to was more than excited to talk about invertebrates. Hopefully I'll make it there again next year, although I doubt I'll have the fortitude to return bugless next time...

## Back home
Back at my flat, we quickly laid out the contents of the starter kit as we were keen to get the *Cubaris m. 'Mandarin'* out of their little plastic tub which had been jostled around on various trains during our journey home.

![Image](/images/kitContents.jpeg){: width="400"}
*Contents of isopod kit*

We laid the soil and added the layer of dried leaves ensuring equal coverage. After this we added the obstacles, moss, and the little feeding tray. The seed pod and bark are great hidey-holes for the isopods and the cuttlefish can be grazed on to provide calcium.

![Image](/images/isopodHome.png){: width="300"}
*Initial finished isopod enclosure*

With the enclosure set up, we watched the *Cubaris m. 'Mandarin'* clamber around in their new home, slowly becoming more familiar until they settled into hidden burrows for some well-deserved rest. 

*Note to those who know what they're doing: We also noticed that we had not set up a moisture gradient correctly and adjusted the layout later on.*
