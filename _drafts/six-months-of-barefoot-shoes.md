Half a year ago, in early April, I bought my first pair of barefoot shoes.
These were a pair of green [Tadeevo's Ease trainers](https://tadeevo.com/en_GB/p/Ease-green-barefoot-shoes-Tadeevo/130) made from a combination of suede and a breathable fabric, with a [stack height of 5mm](https://anyasreviews.com/tadeevo-minimalist-review/) (assuming similar construction to Tadeevo Bliss reviewed by [Anya's Reviews](https://anyasreviews.com)).

![Image](/images/tadeevoEase.png){: width="300" }

Prior to their arrival I researched the transition process and found a few repeated snippets of advice:
- Start slowly. 
  Begin with around 10 minutes per day and gradually increase.
- If you have pain that does not go away by the next day, then rest and return once it is gone.
- Poor form can cause pain. Feel your body carefully and adjust until it stops.
  This may include responding to heel pain by landing with your feet beneath you rather in front.
  
*Note: While using data from your body can be useful, please also use common sense. If you can't explain why you are making an adjustment, you may just need a break and adjusting your form will not help.*

## First impression

When the shoes first arrived from Poland I immediately took them on a 10-minute expedition of the area around my flat.
I found that, although used to walking barefoot indoors, it was exciting to more acutely feel the previously forbidden outdoor ground I'd covered so often in boots, or thick-soled trainers.
By the end of my short walk, I was already fighting the invading ideas of barefoot-dom and Instagram evangelism. 
Despite this, I steeled myself and considered the other thing I'd noticed: that my shoes were a too long, and I'd probably ordered the wrong size.
