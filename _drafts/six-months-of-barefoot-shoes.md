Half a year ago, in early April, I bought my first pair of barefoot shoes.
These were a pair of green [Tadeevo's Ease trainer](https://tadeevo.com/en_GB/p/Ease-green-barefoot-shoes-Tadeevo/130) made from a combination of suede and a breathable fabric with a [stack height of 5mm](https://anyasreviews.com/tadeevo-minimalist-review/) (assuming similar construction to Tadeevo Bliss reviewed by [Anya's Reviews](https://anyasreviews.com)).

![Image](/images/tadeevoEase.png){: width="300" }

Prior to their arrival I researched the transition process and found a few repeated snippets of advice:
- Start slowly. 
  Begin with around 10 minutes per day and gradually increase.
- If you have pain that does not go away by the next day, then rest and return once it has healed.
- Another source of pain is poor form. The advice here is to feel your body carefully and adjust to stop the issue.
  This may include responding to heel pain by landing with your feet beneath you rather than stomping your heel in front of you (obvious example I know).
  
*Note: While using data from your body can be useful, please also use common sense. If you can't explain why you are making an adjustment, you may just need a break and adjusting your form will not help.*

## First impression

When the shoes first arrived from Poland I immediately took them on a 10-minute expedition in the area around my flat.
I found that, although used to walking barefoot indoors, there was something exciting about the feeling of the previously forbidden outdoor ground beneath my feet.
The pavement cracks and the tactile paving around the traffic lights were exhilarating, and I had to fight the emerging ideas of barefoot-dom and Instagram evangelism. 
Despite this, I steeled myself and began to consider the other thing I'd noticed: my shoes were a bit too long, and I had potentially ordered the wrong size.
