[The Amateur Entomologist Society's annual convention](https://www.amentsoc.org/events/exhibitions.html) was recommended to my partner and me by a reptile store employee we'd spoken to the week prior. Easily convinced, the following Saturday we took a train to Kempton Park Racecourse, Surrey, and began our buggy day out.

We entered through a small side-door, and passing the youth club stand, emerged into the convention's main hall, busy with entomologists with their wooden boxes, and marked by a musty smell from the pinned insects and live invertebrates.

For the first hour, we explored the convention, seeing all manner of beetles, moths, butterflies and tarantulas pinned and organised for their selection by hobbyists. Further in, we also found live millipedes, centipedes and the prior mentioned invertebrates also for sale as live pets.

![Image](/images/millipedes.png){: width="300" }
*Box of millipedes, including one that I held*

We took interest in [Isolife's stand](https://isolife.co.uk/) as my partner was looking for isopods for her vampire crab paludarium.
The employees were lovely and directed us to their starter kit and some Cubaris murina 'Mandarin' which we bought planning to breed them up in preparation for them to join her vampire crabs in the paludarium.

![Image](/images/isopod.png){: width="120"}
*One of our new Cubaris murina 'Mandarin'*

I also considered buying one of their millipedes and went to [Venomous Visions' stand](https://www.venomousvisions.co.uk/) to practice handling one of their millipedes to see if I liked it. Although I did like them, I decided it would be best to wait and get a feel for the isopods before embarking on a life of creepy crawlies. 

The next item on the agenda was finding a pinned Atlas moth as a birthday gift for a party the next day. These moths are massive! We found a few sellers and opted for the cheapest from the smaller, but very friendly, stand run by [Bob Dollery](https://www.amentsoc.org/about/news/0257/) upstairs.
